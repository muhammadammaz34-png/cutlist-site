A tool earns a place here only if it helps with a real editing job. This is the process each tool goes through.

The test clips

Each tool is tried on the same kinds of footage, because easy footage makes every tool look good.

  • A noisy interview recorded in an ordinary room.
  • A fast talker who uses names and technical terms.
  • A subject with loose hair against a background of a similar color.
  • A shot with movement and some motion blur.

What gets checked

  • Time. How long the job takes with the tool compared with doing it by hand, including the cleanup afterward.
  • Result. Whether the output holds up on headphones, on a phone speaker, and full screen.
  • Limits. Watermarks, export caps, file length limits, and anything that changes on the free plan.
  • Price. What it costs on the day of the check, and what you actually get for it.

Keeping guides current

Every article shows the date it was last checked. Tools get updated often, so guides are re-checked when a tool changes in a way that affects the steps, and older ones are reviewed regularly. If a guide is out of date, let me know.

Money and independence

Some links on the site may earn a commission, and the site may show ads. Neither decides what gets recommended. The details are on the disclosure page.
