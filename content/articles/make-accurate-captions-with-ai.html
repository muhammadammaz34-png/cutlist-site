Short version. AI gets most of the words right and most of the formatting wrong. Let it transcribe, then spend your time on three things: names and jargon, line breaks, and timing. Watch the whole video once with the sound off before you export.

Choose the caption type first

There are two kinds, and the choice changes how you work.

  • Burned-in captions are part of the picture. Use them for short vertical videos on social platforms, where viewers often watch without sound and you want control over the look.
  • Caption files (SRT or VTT) are uploaded next to the video. Use them for YouTube, Vimeo, and anything where viewers should be able to turn captions on and off. They are also better for accessibility.

Clean the audio first

Transcription accuracy depends heavily on the audio. Hiss, music, and room echo all cause wrong words. If the recording is rough, fix it before you transcribe. The steps are in the guide on fixing a noisy voice recording.

If the tool lets you set the language or accent, set it. The default is often wrong for non-US speakers.

Tools that transcribe

  • Descript, where captions come from the transcript you edit.
  • Premiere Pro, which has speech to text and caption tracks built in.
  • DaVinci Resolve Studio, which can create subtitles from the audio.
  • CapCut, which has auto captions aimed at short social videos.

Features and plan limits change often. Check the current version of the tool before you rely on it for client work.

The three passes

Pass 1: the words

Read the transcript while the video plays. Fix the things AI misses most: people's names, brand names, acronyms, numbers, and technical terms. If the same mistake repeats, use find and replace. Some tools also let you add a custom word list, which saves time on the next video.

Pass 2: the line breaks

Auto captions often split lines in the wrong place. For horizontal video, keep to one or two lines with about 42 characters per line at most. Netflix's public style guide, a common reference, uses the same limit.

Break at natural pauses. Avoid ending a line on a word like "the" or "and", and keep a name or phrase on one line.

Pass 3: the timing

A caption has to stay on screen long enough to read. As a rule, aim for no more than about 20 characters per second, and never leave a caption up for less than a second. If a caption flashes by, merge it with the next one or extend it slightly.

Short vertical videos

Short captions work better here. Show a few words at a time, or one short line. Keep the text clear of the buttons and username area on the bottom and right side of the screen. Check the layout in the platform's own preview, because each app places its interface differently.

Highlighting the word being spoken can help retention, but only if it stays readable. Use one highlight color and keep it consistent.

Before you export

  • Watch the full video once with the sound off. If you can follow it, the captions work.
  • Check the first and last ten seconds, where timing errors are most visible.
  • For burned-in captions, confirm the font and size are readable on a phone.
  • For caption files, upload the SRT or VTT and check it in the platform's player.
